Bioinformatics Lab

Analyse DNA, follow it through transcription and translation, look up codons and see what mutations do to a sequence. All calculations run in your browser.

DNA sequence analyser

Paste a DNA sequence to see its length, base counts, GC content, complement and reverse complement.

Use the letters A, C, G and T. Spaces, line breaks and lower case are ignored, as are FASTA header lines that begin with >.

Results will appear here after you analyse a sequence.

DNA to RNA transcription

Transcription copies a gene into messenger RNA (mRNA). Choose which DNA strand you are entering.

Strand you are entering

Convention. Sequences are always written 5′ → 3′.

The coding strand has the same sequence as the mRNA, with every T replaced by U.

The template strand is read by RNA polymerase, so the mRNA is its reverse complement with U in place of T.

The mRNA sequence will appear here.

RNA to protein translation

Translation reads mRNA three bases (one codon) at a time and looks each codon up in the standard genetic code.

Convention. Bases are counted from 1 at the 5′ end. Frame 1 reads codons starting at base 1, frame 2 at base 2 and frame 3 at base 3. Only the forward strand is read.

Stop codons are shown as “Stop” and written * in the protein. Leftover bases at the end that do not form a full codon are not translated.

The amino-acid chain will appear here.
